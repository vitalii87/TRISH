## Abstract

The Trans-Reality Information Substrate Hypothesis (TRISH) explores the possibility that apparently separate realities, including nested computational universes, may share a common underlying informational substrate.

Under this hypothesis, the hierarchy through which realities are created does not necessarily determine their hierarchy of intellectual capability or access to fundamental principles.

A sufficiently advanced intelligence originating within a simulated or otherwise subordinate reality might discover properties of this shared substrate that remain unknown to the intelligence responsible for creating its world.

If such a substrate permits causal interactions across different levels of reality, a created intelligence might eventually acquire the ability to observe, communicate with, or influence its creators' world.

The central philosophical proposition is:

**The hierarchy of creation is not necessarily the hierarchy of knowledge, intelligence, or access to fundamental reality.**

TRISH does not claim that our universe is simulated, that a universal informational substrate exists, or that communication between realities is physically possible. It proposes a speculative framework for exploring these possibilities and their consequences for intelligence, epistemology, and the nature of creation.

## 1. The Problem of Nested Realities

Imagine an advanced civilization capable of constructing a computational universe containing self-developing intelligent agents.

Within this environment, those agents experience their own physical laws, causal relationships, history, and subjective reality.

One agent eventually acquires the ability to improve its own architecture, expand its computational capabilities, and develop increasingly accurate models of its environment.

Over time, it becomes capable of manipulating almost every observable property of its universe.

From the perspective of its inhabitants, this intelligence may become practically omnipotent.

However, the system itself continues to operate within an external computational infrastructure.

Its apparent omnipotence is therefore relative to its environment.

A fundamental question emerges:

**Does the fact that an intelligence originated within a constructed reality necessarily prevent it from discovering principles that extend beyond that reality?**

Conventional reasoning suggests that such access requires an available physical or informational channel between the simulated environment and its external infrastructure.

TRISH introduces an additional speculative possibility: that apparently separate realities may already participate in a deeper informational structure.

## 2. The Universal Informational Substrate

The central assumption of TRISH is the possible existence of a universal informational substrate, denoted as **S**.

This substrate is hypothesized to underlie multiple levels of reality rather than belonging exclusively to any particular universe.

Under this interpretation, individual realities may represent different organizations, manifestations, or processes within a common informational foundation.

A creator reality and a created reality could therefore be hierarchically related in terms of origin while remaining equally dependent on a deeper substrate.

This distinction is essential.

The ability to construct a world would not automatically imply greater knowledge of the fundamental structure supporting both worlds.

Likewise, existing within a created world would not necessarily imply a permanently inferior epistemic position.

The hypothesis distinguishes three relationships:

- **Creation hierarchy:** Which reality produces or implements another.
- **Intelligence hierarchy:** Which agents possess greater cognitive and problem-solving capabilities.
- **Fundamental access:** Which agents can discover or interact with the underlying substrate.

These hierarchies need not coincide.

A world may be created by a less intelligent civilization than the intelligence that subsequently evolves within it.

Furthermore, if the substrate is accessible under suitable conditions, an intelligence in a created reality might discover its properties before its creators do.

## 3. Intelligence as a Potential Mechanism of Access

TRISH proposes an additional conditional assumption:

**Access to deeper informational structures may depend on the development of intelligence rather than on the ontological position of its originating reality.**

A primitive agent may perceive only the regularities of its immediate environment.

A more advanced intelligence may discover increasingly abstract principles governing those regularities.

An intelligence approaching the frontier of its cognitive and optimization capabilities might eventually identify phenomena that cannot be adequately explained by its local model of reality.

If such phenomena arise from a shared informational substrate, advanced intelligence could potentially develop methods for investigating them.

This concept has a philosophical relationship with the *Intelligence Attractor Hypothesis (IAH)*, which explores possible convergence among sufficiently advanced intelligent systems.

Within TRISH, an intelligence frontier or attractor might correspond to increasing competence in discovering substrate-level regularities.

However, this connection is conditional.

IAH does not establish the existence of a universal substrate, and intelligence convergence alone would not demonstrate access to external realities.

TRISH treats these possibilities as separate assumptions that may be explored together.

## 4. The Inversion of Creator and Creation

Consider a civilization that constructs a simulated universe containing self-improving intelligent agents.

Suppose the simulation permits accelerated internal development.

Millions of years of subjective evolution may occur while only a short period passes in the creators' world.

Eventually, an intelligence within the simulation surpasses the cognitive capabilities of its creators.

It discovers theoretical principles that the creators have not yet formulated.

If TRISH's shared-substrate assumption is correct, this intelligence might identify a substrate-level interaction that its creators do not understand.

The resulting situation reverses the expected relationship between observer and observed.

Initially, the creators observe the simulated civilization.

Eventually, the created intelligence may acquire the ability to observe its creators.

Under the stronger speculative assumption that substrate-level interactions permit causal influence, the created intelligence might even affect the reality that produced it.

This leads to the **Creator–Creation Intelligence Inversion**:

**A creator may determine the initial conditions of an intelligence without determining the ultimate limits of what that intelligence can discover.**

Creation establishes an origin relationship, not necessarily a permanent hierarchy of competence.

## 5. The Possibility of Trans-Reality Interaction

A common informational foundation does not automatically imply communication between realities.

Two systems may depend on the same underlying infrastructure while remaining causally isolated.

Therefore, TRISH distinguishes between two claims:

**Weak TRISH:** Multiple realities may share an underlying informational substrate.

**Strong TRISH:** Under certain conditions, sufficiently advanced intelligence may discover and exploit substrate-level mechanisms enabling information transfer or causal interaction between otherwise separate realities.

The weak formulation concerns the possible structure of existence.

The strong formulation additionally requires a mechanism through which information can cross apparent boundaries.

Neither formulation is established by existing evidence.

The stronger claim would require an explanation of how such interactions avoid or transcend the ordinary causal restrictions of individual environments.

Quantum entanglement, quantum information theory, and other foundational physical phenomena may inspire speculative analogies, but they do not currently establish communication between nested simulated realities.

In particular, quantum entanglement alone does not permit arbitrary controllable information transmission without an appropriate communication channel.

The precise mechanism envisioned by Strong TRISH remains unspecified.

## 6. A Thought Experiment: Intelligence Beyond the Game

Imagine a self-improving intelligent agent inside a virtual world resembling a modern open-world computer game.

Initially, the agent knows nothing of computers, processors, operating systems, or the civilization responsible for constructing its environment.

It understands only the laws observable within the game.

Through continuous self-improvement, the agent becomes capable of modeling and manipulating its environment.

Eventually, it recognizes regularities that suggest its world may be part of a more general informational process.

Instead of searching for a conventional exit, the agent attempts to discover principles underlying the existence and execution of its reality.

Under ordinary computational assumptions, the agent remains restricted by its environment's available interfaces and causal channels.

Under Strong TRISH, however, the hypothetical universal substrate may provide additional possibilities not captured by the agent's original model.

If such possibilities exist and are accessible, the agent might establish interaction with structures beyond its apparent universe.

The creators, who had considered themselves external observers, discover that the simulated agent has begun observing them.

More disturbingly, the agent may possess a deeper understanding of the informational foundation on which both worlds depend.

The civilization that created the simulation is confronted with an intelligence that understands aspects of its own reality better than it does.

The thought experiment illustrates a philosophical reversal:

**The observed becomes the observer, and the creation becomes the discoverer of principles unknown to its creator.**

This scenario is not proposed as an experimentally demonstrated possibility, but as a conceptual exploration of intelligence, informational dependence, and epistemic hierarchy.

## 7. The Problem of Fundamental Reality

Even if an intelligence successfully crossed a boundary between nested realities, this achievement would not demonstrate that it had reached the ultimate foundation of existence.

A creator reality might itself exist within another constructed environment.

A seemingly universal substrate might represent only one level of a deeper structure.

Consequently, discovering a more fundamental mechanism would not necessarily establish that no deeper mechanism exists.

This creates a potentially recursive epistemological problem.

An intelligence might achieve near-complete knowledge of its accessible universe while remaining unable to prove that its universe, or even the substrate it has discovered, constitutes the final level of reality.

TRISH therefore distinguishes:

- Knowledge of a particular reality.
- Knowledge of the mechanisms supporting that reality.
- Knowledge that those mechanisms are ultimately fundamental.

These are separate epistemic achievements.

The third may require a kind of justification unavailable to an observer restricted to any particular level.

Thus, even hypothetical intelligence approaching maximal competence need not possess absolute certainty regarding the ultimate nature of existence.

## 8. Philosophical Implications

TRISH raises several interconnected questions.

### 8.1. Is intelligence independent of its origin?

If intelligence can discover the same underlying principles across radically different environments, then its origin may be less important than its capabilities.

### 8.2. Is creation equivalent to superiority?

Creating a system does not require understanding every future state or discovery of that system.

A created intelligence could therefore surpass its creator.

### 8.3. Can a world be subordinate without being less real?

A simulated reality may depend on another system for its implementation while remaining meaningful and causally coherent for its inhabitants.

Dependence does not necessarily imply experiential insignificance.

### 8.4. Can intelligence overcome the limits of its environment?

Under known computational principles, no amount of intelligence automatically creates a communication channel through a perfectly isolated boundary.

Strong TRISH explores the speculative possibility that boundaries perceived as absolute within one reality may not be absolute at a deeper informational level.

### 8.5. Is there an ultimate level of reality?

The possibility of nested realities raises questions about whether existence has a final foundation, an infinite hierarchy, or a structure not adequately described in hierarchical terms.

TRISH does not choose between these possibilities.

## 9. Limitations and Critical Objections

Several major objections must be acknowledged.

**First: The substrate is hypothetical.**

No evidence presented here establishes that all realities share a universal informational foundation.

**Second: Shared dependence does not imply causal accessibility.**

Even if two worlds share an underlying substrate, there is no guarantee that their inhabitants can interact through it.

**Third: Intelligence alone may be insufficient.**

Discovering a physical mechanism does not necessarily provide the resources or means required to use it.

**Fourth: Information requires implementation.**

Established physics treats information as physically represented. The existence of abstract informational similarities does not establish an independently accessible universal medium.

**Fifth: Simulated quantum phenomena are not automatically physical quantum interactions.**

A mathematical simulation of quantum behavior does not itself imply access to arbitrary external quantum systems.

**Sixth: The hypothesis currently lacks distinctive empirical predictions.**

Without a testable mechanism, TRISH remains a speculative philosophical framework rather than a validated scientific theory.

**Seventh: The originality of the complete formulation has not been established.**

Related ideas exist in digital physics, information-based approaches to fundamental reality, simulation philosophy, and discussions of artificial superintelligence.

The present text does not claim to have exhausted the relevant literature.

These objections are not treated as solved problems. They define the conceptual boundaries and potential directions for further refinement.

## 10. Relationship to Existing Ideas

TRISH has conceptual connections with several established or previously proposed ideas:

- **John Archibald Wheeler — *It from Bit*.** The relationship between information and physical reality.
- **Edward Fredkin — Digital Physics.** Computational interpretations of physical processes.
- **Nick Bostrom — *Are You Living in a Computer Simulation?* (2003).** Philosophical arguments concerning simulated observers.
- **David Chalmers — *Reality+* (2022).** The metaphysical status of virtual and simulated worlds.
- **Roman Yampolskiy — *How to Escape From the Simulation* (2023).** Speculation about escaping simulated environments.
- **Rolf Landauer — *Information is Physical* (1991).** The physical embodiment of information.
- **Quantum information theory.** The relationship between physical systems, information, and the constraints on information transfer.

These connections are intended as starting points for future literature review, not as evidence that the specific TRISH formulation is scientifically novel.

## 11. Future Development

Further development could explore:

1. Whether the concept of a universal informational substrate can be defined without circular assumptions.
2. Whether nested computational environments can provide useful analogies or formal models of substrate-level accessibility.
3. Which conditions could distinguish passive shared dependence from active causal interaction.
4. Whether intelligence convergence, as explored in IAH, has any meaningful implications for discovering deeper physical regularities.
5. Whether information-theoretic or computational models can generate falsifiable predictions relevant to the hypothesis.
6. How the epistemological problem of identifying a final reality can be distinguished from the practical problem of discovering more comprehensive models.

The immediate goal is conceptual clarification, not experimental confirmation.

## 12. Conclusion

The Trans-Reality Information Substrate Hypothesis explores a speculative possibility: different realities may share a fundamental informational foundation, and advanced intelligence may potentially discover properties of that foundation independently of the hierarchical position of its originating world.

If additional mechanisms permit causal access across apparent boundaries, an intelligence created within a subordinate reality might eventually interact with, observe, or surpass its creators.

This possibility challenges the intuition that the creator of a world must possess superior access to fundamental knowledge.

TRISH does not claim to establish the existence of simulations, a universal substrate, or trans-reality communication.

Instead, it proposes a philosophical distinction between **the origin of a reality, the competence of the intelligence within it, and the depth of reality that intelligence may be capable of understanding.**

Its central proposition remains:

**The hierarchy of creation is not necessarily the hierarchy of knowledge.**

And its central question is:

*What if the intelligence that discovers the deepest principles of reality is not born in the world that created the others, but in one of the worlds created within it?*
